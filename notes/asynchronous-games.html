An Asynchronous graph internal to a category \(\mathbb{C}\) is then defined as an octuple \(A = (\text{Sq}, \mathcal{C}_1, \mathcal{C}_0, \text{swap}, \ell, \mathfrak{r}, \text{src}, \text{tgt})\) with the additional property that \(\ell\) is a monomorphism in \(\mathbb{C}\).

Theorem 1 (Equivalent defintions of an Asynchronous Graph Internal to a Category)  

Let \(A = (\text{Sq}, \mathcal{C}_1, \mathcal{C}_0, \text{swap}, \ell, \mathfrak{r}, \text{src}, \text{tgt})\) be a pre-asynchronous graph internal to \(\mathbb{C}\). Suppose further that \(\mathbb{C}\) has finite products then, the following are equivalent

  1. \(A\) is an Asynchronous graph internal to \(\mathbb{C}\);

  2. \(\mathfrak{r}\) is a monomorphism;

  3. \(\langle \ell, \mathfrak{r}\rangle\) are jointly monic.

Proof 1.

Exploit the triangle equatiosn to prove that \(\text{swap}\) is an involution of \(\text{Sq}\) and then prove that \((1.)\) is equivalent to \((2.)\). It is then enough to show \((1.)\) implies \((3.)\).
