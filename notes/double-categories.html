Definitions

Definition 1 (Category) A Category \(C\) consists of the following data:

  1. First class \(C_0\) and another set \(C_1\);

  2. Functions \(s, t: C_1 \rightarrow C_0\) (the source and target functions);

  3. A function \(\text{id} : C_0 \rightarrow C_1\) (the function which picks out an identity) such that \(\text{id}\) equalises \(s\) and \(t\);

  4. For every \(f, g \in C_1\) such that \(t(f) = s(g)\) there is a unique element (there composition) \(g \circ f \in C_1\) where \(s(g\circ f) = s(f)\) and \(t(g \circ f) = t(g)\).

This data is required to satisfy:

For every \(f \in C_1\), we have that \(f \circ \text{id}(s(f)) = f\) and \(\text{id}(t(f)) \circ f = f\);

For every \(f, g, h \in C_1\), we have that \((h\circ g) \circ f = h \circ (g \circ f)\).

Definition 2 (Double Category) A Double Category \(\mathbb{C}\) consists of the following data:

  1. a Category \(\ \mathbb{C}_0 \ \), whose objects we call 0-cells, and whose arrows we call tight 1-cells \(f: V \rightarrow V'\);

  2. a Category \(\ \mathbb{C}_1 \ \), whose objects we call loose 1-cells \(M: V \not\rightarrow W\), and whose arrows we call 2-cells

  1. two functors \(\text{src}, \text{tgt} : \mathbb{C}_1 \rightarrow \mathbb{C}_0\) called source and target respectively;

  2. composition and identity functors \(\circ : \mathbb{C}_1 \times_{\mathbb{C}_0} \mathbb{C}_1 \rightarrow \mathbb{C}_1\) and \(\text{id} : \mathbb{C}_0 \rightarrow \mathbb{C}_1\);

  3. natural families of isomorphisms in \(\mathbb{C}_1\):

Several questions come about when staring at the above definition. If arrows are objects in \(\mathbb{C}_1\) what gives the right to state that they look like arrows with sources and targets that could just as well be source and targets of arrows in \(\mathbb{C}_0\). The source and target functors are lacking defining equations they should be compatible with the composition and identity functors in the way that is implied from the mantra “double categories are category objects in the category of categories”. In particular, the identity functor must equalise the source and target functors meaning that enrichment is ill-defined or based on evil equality. The more significant problem is that the presentation of squares need to have a specific orientation which produces conflicts in the literature.

A better definition starts from finding a better definition for categories.

Definition 3 (Category, indexed version) A category is the data of:

  1. A class \(C_0\);

  2. For every \(A, B \in C_0\) a set \(C_1(A,B)\);

  3. For every \(A \in C_0\), a distinguished element \(\text{id}_A \in C_1(A,A)\);

  4. For every \(A, B, C \in C_0\), and every \(f \in C_1(A, B)\), as well as \(g \in C_1(B,C)\) there is a \(g \circ f \in C_1(A,C)\);

satisfying the requirements that

For every \(A, B \in C_0\), and every \(f \in C_1(A, B)\) the following equations hold

\[f \circ \text{id}_A = f = \text{id}_B \circ f.\]

For every \(A, B, C, D \in C_0\), and every \(f \in C_1(A, B)\) and also every \(g \in C_1(B,C)\) as well as \(h \in C_1(C, D)\), the following equation holds

\[(h \circ g) \circ f = h \circ (g \circ f).\]

The sets \(C_1(A,B)\) are pairwise disjoint.

It can be shown through a laborious formal argument that the two definitions, the version with source and target and the indexed version are equivalent and thus we can use the capitalised or uncapitalised interchangeably. We would like to have a similar definition for double categories as for the definition of category. The reason to do this is so that there is no confusion in the definition and data can be expressed clearly.

Definition 4 (Double category, indexed version) A double category \(\mathbb{C}\) consists of the following data:

  1. A class of objects \(\mathcal{O}\) whose members are called the 0-cells of \(\mathbb{C}\) (generically upper roman from the end of the alphabet);

  2. For every \(V, V' \in \mathcal{O}\) a class \(\text{hom}_{\tau}(V,V')\), called tight arrows (generically lower roman from the beginning of the alphabet);

  3. For every \(V, W \in \mathcal{O}\) a class \(\text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,W)\) called loose arrows (generically upper roman from middle of the alphabet);

  4. For every \(V \in \mathcal{O}\) a distinguished tight arrow \(1_V \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V,V)\);

  5. For every \(V \in \mathcal{O}\) a distinguished loose arrow \(id_V \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,V)\);

  6. For every \(V, V', V'' \in \mathcal{O}\), and every \(f \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V,V')\) and also every \(f' \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V',V'')\), there is a \(f \frown f' \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V,V'')\);

  7. For every \(V, W, V', W' \in \mathcal{O}\), and every \(f \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V,V'), g \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(W,W')\) and also every \(M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,W), M' \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V',W')\), there is a set \(\text{wit}(V, W, V', W', f, g, M, M')\) called witnesses (generically lower greek)

  1. For every \(V, W \in \mathcal{O}\), and every \(M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,W)\) there is a distinguished witness \(\upsilon_M \in \text{wit}(V, W, V, W, 1_V, 1_W, M, M)\);
  1. For every \(V, W, V', W', V'', W'' \in \mathcal{O}\), and every \(f \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V,V'), f' \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V',V''), g \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(W,W'), g' \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(W',W'')\) and also every \(M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,W), M' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V',W'), M''\in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V'',W'')\) as well as every \(\phi \in \text{wit}(V, W, V', W', f, g, M, M'), \phi' \in \text{wit}(V', W', V'', W'', f', g', M', M'')\) there is a \(\phi \smile \phi' \in \text{wit}(V, W, V'', W'', f \frown f', g \frown g', M, M'')\).

  1. For every \(V, W, X \in \mathcal{O}\) and every \(M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,W), N \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(W,X)\) there is a \(N \circ M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,X)\);

  2. For every \(V, W, X, V', W', X' \in \mathcal{O}\) and every \(f \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V,V'), g \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(W,W'), h \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(X,X')\) and also every \(M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,W), N \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(W,X), M' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V',W'), N' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(W',X')\) as well as every witness \(\phi \in \text{wit}(V, W, V', W', f, g, M, M')\) and \(\psi \in \text{wit}(W, X, W', X', g, h, N, N')\) then there is a witness \(\psi \star \phi \in \text{wit}(V, X, V', X', f, h, N \circ M, N' \circ M')\).

  3. For every \(V, W \in \mathcal{O}\) and every \(M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,W)\) there is a witness \(\ell_M \in \text{wit}(V, W, V, W, 1_V, 1_W, id_W \circ M, M)\). The witness is natural in \(M\) in the sense that: for every \(M' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,W)\) and every \(\phi \in \text{wit}(V, W, V, W, 1_V, 1_W, M, M')\) we have

\[\ell_M \smile \phi = (\upsilon_{id_W} \star \phi) \smile \ell_{M'}.\]

Each \(\ell_M\) also comes equipped with a friend \(k_M \in \text{wit}(V, W, V, W, 1_V, 1_W, M, id_W \circ M)\) such that \(k_M \smile \ell_M = \upsilon_M\) and \(\ell_M \smile k_M = \upsilon_{id_W \circ M}\);

  1. For every \(V, W \in \mathcal{O}\) and every \(M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,W)\) there is a witness \(r_M \in \text{wit}(V, W, V, W, 1_V, 1_W, M \circ id_V, M)\). The witness is natural in \(M\) in the sense that: for every \(M' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,W)\) and every \(\phi \in \text{wit}(V, W, V, W, 1_V, 1_W, M, M')\) we have

\[r_M \smile \phi = (\phi \star \upsilon_{id_V}) \smile r_{M'}\]

Each \(r_M\) comes equipped with a friend \(q_M \in \text{wit}(V, W, V, W, 1_V, 1_W, M, M \circ id_V)\) such that \(q_M \smile r_M = \upsilon_M\) and \(r_M \smile q_M = \upsilon_{M \circ id_V}\);

  1. For every \(V, W, X, Y \in \mathcal{O}\) and every \(P \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(X,Y), N \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(W,X), M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V,W)\) there is a witness \(a_{P, N, M} \in \text{wit}(V, Y, V, Y, 1_V, 1_Y, (P \circ N) \circ M, P \circ (N \circ M))\). This witness is natural in \(P, N\) and \(M\) simultaneously meaning that: for every \(P' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(X, Y), N' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(W, X), M' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V, W)\) and every \(\psi \in \text{wit}(X, Y, X, Y, 1_X, 1_Y, P, P'), \chi \in \text{wit}(W, X, W, X, 1_W, 1_X, N, N'), \phi \in \text{wit}(V, W, V, W, 1_V, 1_W, M, M')\) we have

\[((\psi \star \upsilon_{id_N}) \star \upsilon_{id_M})\smile a_{P', N, M} = a_{P, N, M} \smile (\psi \star (\upsilon_{id_N} \star \upsilon_{id_M})),\]

\[((\upsilon_{id_P} \star \chi) \star \upsilon_{id_M}) \smile a_{P, N', M} = a_{P, N, M} \smile (\upsilon_{id_P} \star (\chi \star \upsilon_{id_M})),\]

\[((\upsilon_{id_P} \star \upsilon_{id_N}) \star \phi) \smile a_{P, N, M'} = a_{P, N, M} \smile (\upsilon_{id_P} \star (\upsilon_{id_N} \star \phi)) \]

Each \(a_{P, N, M}\) comes equipped with a friend \(b_{P, N, M} \in \text{wit}(V, Y, V, Y, 1_V, 1_Y, P \circ (N \circ M)), (P \circ N) \circ M)\) such that \(a_{P,N,M} \smile b_{P, N, M} = \upsilon_{(P \circ N) \circ M}\) and \(b_{P, N, M} \smile a_{P, N, M} = \upsilon_{P \circ (N \circ M)}\).

This data is required to satisfy the following:

For every \(V, V' \in \mathcal{O}\) and every \(f \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V,V')\) we have

\[1_V \frown f = f = f \frown 1_{V'};\]

For every \(V, W, V', W', V'', W'', V''', W''' \in \mathcal{O}\) and every \(M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V, W), M' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V', W'), M'' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V'', W''), M''' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V''', W''')\) and also every \(f \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V, V'), g \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(W,W'), f' \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V', V''), g' \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(W',W''), f'' \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V'', V'''), g'' \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(W'',W''')\) as well as \(\phi \in \text{wit}(V, W, V', W', f, g, M, M'), \phi' \in \text{wit}(V', W', V'', W'', f', g', M', M''), \phi'' \in \text{wit}(V'', W'', V''', W''', f'', g'', M'', M''')\) we have

\[ f'' \frown (f' \frown f) = (f'' \frown f') \frown f,\]

\[ \phi'' \smile (\phi' \smile \phi) = (\phi'' \smile \phi') \smile \phi;\]

For every \(V, W, V', W' \in \mathcal{O}\) and every \(M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V, W), M' \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V',W')\) and also every \(f \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(V,V'), g \in \text{hom}_{\tau}(W,W')\) as well as every witness \(\phi \in \text{wit}(V, W, V', W', f, g, M, M')\) we have

\[\upsilon_M \smile \phi = \phi = \phi \smile \upsilon_{M'};\]

For every \(V, W, X, Y, Z \in \mathcal{O}\) and every \(Q \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(Y,Z), P \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(X,Y), N \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(W, X), M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V, W)\) we have

\[(a_{Q, P, N} \star id_M) \smile a_{Q, P \circ N, M} \smile (id_Q \star a_{P, N, M}) = a_{Q \circ P, N, M} \smile a_{Q, P, N \circ M};\]

For every \(V, W, X \in \mathcal{O}\) and every \(N \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(W, X), M \in \text{hom}_{\lambda}(V, W)\) we have

\[a_{N, id_W, M} \smile (id_M \star \ell_N) = r_M \star id_N.\]
